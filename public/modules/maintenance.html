Before Caspian ERP

The maintenance system knows the machine and nothing else

A standalone CMMS has its own asset list, its own people list and its own spares list. Keeping three lists in step is the actual job.

Breakdowns are reported by phone

A request arrives as a call, gets written on a board, and its history exists only in the memory of whoever took it.

Preventive plans slip quietly

The plan says quarterly. The last one was seven months ago. Nothing raised its hand, because nothing was watching.

Spares come off the shelf uncounted

The technician takes what they need. Stock is wrong, the job cost is wrong, and the next planner works from both.

What it does

Everything the equipment needs, in one register

Reactive work, planned work, calibration and warranty — against the same assets.

An asset register that carries its history

Every piece of equipment as a record: what it is, where it is, and everything that has happened to it.

  • Assets with location, criticality and documents
  • Full work history against the asset, not against a person’s memory
  • Vehicles and production equipment in the same register
  • Failure codes so patterns can actually be seen

Requests and work orders

Reactive work raised by anyone, triaged, assigned and closed with what was done.

  • Requests raised from the field on a phone
  • Triaged into scheduled work with an owner and a priority
  • Parts issued from stock as part of closing the job
  • The job cost built from labour and material actually used

Preventive plans that raise their own work

Time-based and meter-based plans that generate the work rather than relying on a reminder.

  • Preventive plans per asset with interval and scope
  • Meter readings driving usage-based servicing
  • Maintenance checklists attached to the planned job
  • Overdue preventive work visible before it becomes a breakdown

Calibration with certificates

Instruments tracked with their calibration status, due dates and certificates.

  • Calibration schedule per instrument
  • Certificates stored against the asset
  • Out-of-calibration equipment flagged before use
  • The evidence a client or auditor asks for, on demand

Warranty claims that get made

Warranty status held with the asset, so claims happen while they are still valid.

  • Warranty periods recorded per asset
  • Claims raised against the supplier and tracked
  • Linked to the failure that triggered them
  • Money recovered rather than quietly written off

Meter readings and failure analysis

Usage and failure data captured routinely, so reliability work has something to stand on.

  • Meter readings recorded in the field
  • Failure codes applied consistently at closure
  • Repeat failures visible per asset and per code
  • Preventive intervals adjusted from evidence

Maintenance questions we get asked

Do preventive plans create work automatically?
Yes — plans generate preventive actions on their interval, by date or by meter reading, so overdue servicing surfaces on its own rather than depending on someone’s reminder.
Are vehicles maintained here or in Logistics?
Here. A vehicle is an asset, so its servicing, meter readings and preventive plans run through Maintenance. Logistics handles the journeys it makes and the documents it carries.
Can technicians work from a phone in the field?
Yes. Requests, job lists, checklists and meter readings are all designed to be used in a phone browser, which is where most of this work actually happens.
Does issuing a spare part update inventory?
Yes. Parts issued to a work order are real stock movements against the warehouse, which is what keeps stock accurate and gives the job its true material cost.
Can we track calibration certificates for client audits?
Calibration schedules, statuses and certificates are held against the instrument, so producing evidence is a filter rather than a search through folders.
