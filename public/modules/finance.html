Before Caspian ERP

Accounting is a second data-entry job

The operation records what happened. Then someone types the same events into the accounting package, a few weeks later, from PDFs.

Bills are entered from paperwork

A vendor invoice arrives as a PDF, and someone re-keys lines that already exist as a purchase order and a goods receipt in another system.

Budget versus actual arrives too late to act on

By the time the month is closed and the report is assembled, the overspend it reveals is two months old.

Expenses go in on paper and come back in arguments

Claims are submitted on forms, approved by whoever is around, and reconciled against receipts nobody can find.

What it does

Proper finance, connected to the work that generated it

A chart of accounts, budgets and reports — with the transactions arriving from the modules that created them.

Chart of accounts and bank accounts

A real accounting structure, not a category field bolted onto an operations tool.

  • Chart of accounts you define, matching your reporting
  • Bank accounts with balances and reconciliation
  • Consistent treatment across every module that posts
  • Multi-organization separation, with no data crossing the boundary

Invoices, bills and payments

Receivables and payables in one place, arriving from the operation rather than from a pile of paper.

  • Customer invoices raised from sales orders in CRM
  • Vendor bills arriving matched to order and receipt
  • Payments recorded against invoices and bills
  • Outstanding position visible without a spreadsheet

Expenses with an approval trail

Claims submitted, approved and posted, with the evidence attached.

  • Expenses submitted by the person who incurred them
  • Approval routed by your own rules
  • Receipts attached to the claim, not to an envelope
  • Posted to the right account without re-keying

Budgets that are checked while there is time

Budgets per account and period, compared against actuals as they happen.

  • Budgets per account, department and period
  • Actual against budget available continuously
  • Overspend visible during the month, not after it
  • The same figures the KPI dashboard reads

Tax rates handled consistently

Tax treatment defined once and applied everywhere a document is raised.

  • Tax rates configured for your jurisdiction
  • Applied consistently to quotes, invoices and bills
  • Reportable, because the treatment is structured
  • No per-document improvisation

Reports from live records

Financial reporting that reads the ledger rather than an export of it.

  • Standard financial reports from live data
  • Drill from a figure toward the transactions behind it
  • Period comparisons without rebuilding a spreadsheet
  • Reconciles with the operation because it shares its records

Finance questions we get asked

Is this a replacement for our statutory accounting package?
It is a working ledger for running the business — accounts, invoices, bills, expenses, payments, budgets, tax and reporting. Whether it replaces your statutory filing package depends on your jurisdiction and your accountant, and that is a conversation worth having before you switch.
Do vendor bills really arrive matched?
Yes, when the purchase went through Procurement. The bill references the purchase order and the goods receipt, so the three-way match is a verification rather than a reconstruction from PDFs.
Can we set our own chart of accounts?
Yes. The chart of accounts is defined by you, and every module that posts uses it, which is what keeps operational and financial reporting consistent.
Who can see financial data?
Only the roles you grant it to, enforced server-side. Finance is the module most organizations restrict hardest, and the permission model supports that down to the individual tool.
How does budget versus actual stay current?
Because actuals come from documents raised by the operation as it works — bills, expenses and invoices — rather than from a monthly import. You see consumption during the period, while there is still time to respond.
