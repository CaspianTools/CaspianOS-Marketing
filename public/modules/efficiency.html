Before CaspianOS

The work is real; the tracking is scattered

Most organizations do not lack systems. They lack one place where a person can see everything that is theirs.

Actions live in five places

One in an incident report, one in meeting minutes, one in a message, two in someone’s head. None of them on a single list with a due date.

Meetings produce minutes, not movement

Actions are agreed, written into a document, and then depend entirely on someone remembering to read that document again.

Improvement ideas evaporate

The best suggestions come from the shop floor, get mentioned once, and are never recorded anywhere that survives the shift.

What it does

One working surface for everything that is yours

This is the module that gets people into the system on a quiet week — which is what makes the other modules work.

Tasks with owners and due dates

A single list per person, fed by the whole platform.

  • Priorities, due dates, assignees and a board view
  • Actions from incidents, investigations and meetings arrive here
  • Overdue work surfaced on the home screen, not buried
  • Assigned to a person, never to a department

Meetings that end in tracked actions

Agenda, attendance and outcomes held together, with actions that become tasks.

  • Agendas prepared in advance and visible to attendees
  • Attendance recorded against personnel records
  • Actions assigned during the meeting, owned afterwards
  • Previous meetings searchable rather than filed

Forms, including for people outside the system

Build a form, share a public link, and collect structured responses.

  • Custom forms built without a developer
  • Shareable public links for contractors, visitors and clients
  • Responses stored as records you can filter and export
  • No licence needed for the person filling it in

Checklists, notes and a personal diary

The everyday scaffolding — routine checks, working notes and a log of what happened.

  • Reusable checklists for routine and pre-job checks
  • Notes kept with the work rather than in a separate app
  • A personal diary for the record you always wish you had kept
  • All of it searchable from the same global search

Lean management register

Improvement ideas captured, assessed and tracked to implementation.

  • Suggestions recorded from anyone, not just management
  • Assessed, prioritised and given an owner
  • Progress visible so contributors see what happened to their idea
  • A trail of what changed and what it was worth

One calendar across the platform

Meetings, sessions, leave and scheduled work in a single view.

  • Training sessions from HR appear alongside meetings
  • Planned maintenance and journeys visible in the same place
  • Personal and team views
  • Nothing to sync, because there is nothing separate

Efficiency questions we get asked

Why would we use this if we already have a task app?
Because a separate task app cannot see your incidents, work orders or projects. The value here is not the task list itself — it is that an action raised during an investigation or a meeting lands on the same list without anyone re-typing it.
Can people outside our organization fill in a form?
Yes. Forms can be published with a shareable public link, so contractors, visitors and clients can submit without an account or a licence. Responses come back as structured records.
Is the diary visible to managers?
The diary is designed as a personal log, and visibility is controlled by the same permission model as everything else. Most organizations keep it private to its owner.
Does the calendar show more than meetings?
Yes — training sessions, leave, planned maintenance and journeys can appear in the same view, because they are records in the same system rather than entries in separate calendars.
Can we start with just this module?
Many organizations do. Efficiency is the usual entry point because everyone can use it on day one, and the operational modules are switched on once people are in the habit of opening the platform.
