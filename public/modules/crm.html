Before Caspian ERP

The CRM stops exactly where the work starts

A deal is marked won, and then somebody re-types the whole thing into the system that actually delivers it.

Quotes are built in a word processor

Prices come from a spreadsheet, the layout comes from last time, and nothing that goes out of the door is connected to what you can actually supply.

Won deals are re-keyed

The order is entered again into operations, with a fresh chance to get a quantity, a price or a delivery date wrong.

The forecast is a conversation

Pipeline value depends on which version of the spreadsheet you opened and how optimistic the last update was.

What it does

From first contact to a sales order the operation can act on

The commercial side of the same platform, using the same items, people and ledger.

Contacts and the relationship around them

Customers and the people inside them, with the history in one place.

  • Contact and company records with owners
  • Activity history against the relationship, not in an inbox
  • Visible to the people who need it, restricted where you want
  • Import from your existing list

Deals with a pipeline that means something

Opportunities with stage, value and expected close, updated by the people who own them.

  • Deals with stage, value, probability and owner
  • Pipeline view by stage and by owner
  • Activities logged against the deal as it progresses
  • Won deals hand over to a sales order without re-keying

Quotes built from the real catalogue

Quotes assembled from the same items the rest of the platform uses.

  • Line items from the shared item catalogue

  • Versioned, so you know what the customer actually received
  • Converted to a sales order on acceptance

Sales orders the operation can deliver against

The accepted order becomes the instruction to produce, pick or ship.

  • Sales orders with lines, dates and status
  • Visible to production and the warehouse, not just to sales
  • Stock availability checked against real warehouse records
  • Invoiced in Finance from the same order

Forecast and analytics

A pipeline forecast built from the deals people are actually working.

  • Forecast by period, stage and owner
  • Conversion analytics across the pipeline
  • Win and loss visible per owner and per customer
  • The same numbers the KPI dashboard reads

Contact to cash, without a re-key

Each document is created from the last, and the operation sees the order as soon as it is won.

CRM questions we get asked

Does a won deal create the operational work?
A won deal becomes a sales order, and the sales order is visible to the warehouse and production directly. That is the point of running the CRM inside the ERP — nobody re-types the order into a second system.
Where do quote prices come from?
From the shared item catalogue that procurement, inventory and finance also use. That is what keeps what you quoted, what you shipped and what you invoiced consistent.
Can we check stock before promising a date?
Yes. Availability comes from actual warehouse records rather than a separately maintained figure, so a promised date can be based on what is really on the shelf.
Is the forecast something the team has to submit?
No. It is built from the deals people are already updating, with stage and probability. If nobody updates their deals the forecast will say so, which is itself useful.
Can sales see operational data, or only their own?
Whatever you decide. Every tool carries its own permissions, so an account manager can be given visibility of stock and order status without access to finance or HR.
