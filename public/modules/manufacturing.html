Before CaspianOS

The plan assumes material the shelf does not have

Production planning built on a stock figure from another system is planning on a rumour.

The BOM lives in a spreadsheet

Component lists are maintained separately from the item catalogue, so a change in one is a silent divergence from the other.

Work orders do not consume anything

Material is drawn for a job and stock is corrected later, which means neither the job cost nor the stock figure is right in between.

Quality results sit apart from the batch

The inspection record exists, but linking it back to what was produced and from which lot is a manual exercise.

What it does

A production module that shares the operation’s data

Small and focused by design — it uses the item catalogue, the stock records and the people the rest of the platform already holds.

Work orders that consume real stock

A job with its material, its line and its status, drawing from the same warehouse records as everything else.

  • Work orders with quantity, dates and status
  • Components issued from stock as a real movement
  • Assigned to a production line and to people
  • Job cost built from material actually consumed

Bills of materials on the shared catalogue

BOM components are catalogue items, so availability is a fact rather than an assumption.

  • Multi-component bills built from the shared item catalogue
  • Availability checked against actual warehouse stock
  • A shortage is visible before the job is released
  • One change to the item propagates everywhere it is used

Production lines with visible load

Define the lines you run and see what is scheduled on each.

  • Production lines as records with their own capacity view
  • Work orders scheduled against a line
  • Current load visible before you promise a date
  • Downtime linked to maintenance rather than guessed at

Quality control against the output

Inspection results attached to what was produced and the lot it came from.

  • QC checks recorded against the work order
  • Pass, fail and rework captured rather than described
  • Traceable to the inventory lot the material came from
  • Non-conformances can raise HSEQ actions with owners

Manufacturing questions we get asked

Is this a full MES?
No. It is the production layer of an ERP: work orders, bills of materials, lines and quality control, sharing the item catalogue and stock with the rest of the platform. It does not do machine-level shop-floor control.
Does a work order actually reduce stock?
Yes. Issuing components to a work order creates real stock movements against the warehouse, which is why the job cost and the stock figure stay consistent with each other.
Can we see whether we have the components before releasing?
Yes — the bill of materials is built from the shared catalogue, so availability is checked against actual warehouse stock rather than an assumed quantity.
How does quality control link to traceability?
QC results are recorded against the work order, and where components are lot-tracked in Inventory, the output traces back to the lots consumed.
What if we only assemble occasionally?
Then switch the module on and use work orders alone. Every tool can be enabled individually, so a workshop that assembles a few units a month does not need to adopt production lines and BOM depth it will not use.
